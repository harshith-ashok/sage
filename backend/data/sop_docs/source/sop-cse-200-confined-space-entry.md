# SOP-CSE-200: Confined Space Entry Safety Procedure

**Document owner:** HSE Department
**Effective date:** 2024-11-01
**Review cycle:** 12 months

## Section 1. Purpose and Scope

This procedure establishes the minimum requirements for safe entry into permit-required confined spaces, including but not limited to storage tanks, pressure vessels, pits, sumps, and sewers. It applies to all employees and contractors performing confined space entry work on site.

A confined space is classified as permit-required if it contains, or has the potential to contain, a hazardous atmosphere, a material that could engulf an entrant, an internal configuration that could trap or asphyxiate an entrant, or any other recognized serious safety or health hazard.

## Section 2. Pre-Entry Requirements

Clause 2.1 — A Confined Space Entry Permit (Form HSE-007) shall be completed and authorized by the Area Supervisor before any entry occurs. The permit is valid for a single shift only and shall not be extended without re-verification of all pre-entry conditions.

Clause 2.2 — Atmospheric testing shall be performed prior to entry and shall confirm: oxygen concentration between 19.5 percent and 23.5 percent, flammable gas below 10 percent of the Lower Explosive Limit (LEL), and toxic gas concentrations (H2S, CO) below the site-specific exposure limits listed in Appendix A.

Clause 2.3 — If atmospheric testing indicates conditions outside the acceptable range in Clause 2.2, entry is prohibited until the space has been purged, ventilated, and re-tested. A minimum of two consecutive passing readings, taken 5 minutes apart, is required before entry is authorized.

Clause 2.4 — All energy sources connected to the confined space (electrical, mechanical, hydraulic, pneumatic, or process piping) shall be isolated per SOP-LOTO-400 prior to entry.

## Section 3. Entry Team Roles and Responsibilities

Step 3.1 — Every confined space entry requires a minimum entry team of three: one Entrant, one Attendant, and one Entry Supervisor. The Attendant shall remain outside the space at all times and shall never enter the space to attempt a rescue.

Step 3.2 — The Entry Supervisor is responsible for verifying that all permit conditions are met, authorizing the permit, and having the authority to cancel the permit if conditions change.

Step 3.3 — The Attendant shall maintain continuous communication with the Entrant (visual, voice, or signal line) and shall monitor atmospheric conditions continuously using a calibrated four-gas monitor.

Step 3.4 — All entry team members shall complete Confined Space Entry training within the last 12 months, documented in the site Training Management System.

## Section 4. Continuous Atmospheric Monitoring

Clause 4.1 — Continuous atmospheric monitoring is mandatory for the duration of the entry. Monitor alarms shall be set at: oxygen below 19.5 percent or above 23.5 percent, LEL above 10 percent, H2S above 10 ppm, and CO above 35 ppm.

Clause 4.2 — Upon any alarm activation, all entrants shall evacuate the space immediately. Re-entry is prohibited until the cause of the alarm has been identified and corrected, and fresh atmospheric testing confirms conditions are within acceptable limits per Section 2.

Clause 4.3 — Gas monitors shall be bump-tested daily before use and calibrated per manufacturer specification, with calibration records retained for 12 months.

## Section 5. Rescue and Emergency Procedures

Step 5.1 — A rescue plan specific to the confined space shall be documented on the entry permit before work begins, including the method of retrieval (mechanical retrieval system for vertical entries exceeding 5 feet) and the location of the nearest rescue team.

Step 5.2 — Non-entry rescue using a retrieval line and mechanical device is the preferred method and shall be used whenever the physical configuration of the space allows it.

Step 5.3 — In the event of an emergency, the Attendant shall immediately notify emergency services and the site Control Room and shall not enter the space under any circumstances, consistent with Step 3.1.

## Section 6. Permit Closure

Clause 6.1 — Upon completion of work, the Entry Supervisor shall verify all entrants have exited, confirm tools and equipment are removed, and formally close the permit.

Clause 6.2 — Closed permits shall be retained by the HSE Department for a minimum of 5 years and are subject to review during incident investigations and regulatory audits.
